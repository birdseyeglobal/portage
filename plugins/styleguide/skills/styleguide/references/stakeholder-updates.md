# Stakeholder Updates and Presentations

Stakeholder updates compress deeper work for decision-makers. They are not full
documentation, source archives, or project journals. Their job is to make
status, risk, decisions, and asks clear enough for the next conversation or
decision.

The format can be a Slack note, email, memo, issue comment, meeting brief,
PDF, or deck. The invariant is compression.

Lead with:

- What changed.
- What matters now.
- What is blocked or risky.
- What decision, support, or attention is needed.

## Compression

Use deliberate fidelity loss. Do not carry every source detail into the update.
Choose the organizing axis the reader needs:

- decision to make;
- risk to understand;
- status change since the last update;
- business or customer impact;
- dependency or sequencing issue;
- tradeoff between options.

Avoid organizing by internal source shape unless that is the thing the reader
is reviewing. A pull request list, research dump, metric table, or roadmap
inventory is source material. Convert it into the status, decision, risk, or
ask it supports.

## Provenance

Preserve provenance without flooding the update. Name the strongest source
behind important claims: a canonical doc, metric, incident, customer example,
research note, design decision, pull request, roadmap item, or owner update.

Prefer one precise link over a pile of weak links. If the reader may need to
audit the claim, make the source reachable.

## Topic Shape

For each topic, use only the parts that are useful:

- point;
- why it matters;
- evidence;
- risk or tradeoff;
- decision or ask;
- owner or next state.

Not every topic needs every part. Do not turn this into a template when a
shorter update works.

## Presentation Surfaces

Some stakeholder updates include a presentation deck, but many do not. When
there is a separate presentation surface, split the work:

- The audience-facing surface carries the compressed claim path.
- Companion notes carry the deeper source trail, caveats, and presenter logic.

Slides should not become documentation. Use one main claim per slide and prefer
diagrams, timelines, maps, and comparisons over dense paragraphs.

## Freshness Pass

Before sending, verify the facts most likely to drift:

- dates and relative time references;
- owner and status;
- metrics and counts;
- links;
- blockers;
- open decisions and asks.

If the update mentions current work, confirm that the status still matches the
latest source of truth.
